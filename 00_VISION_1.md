## 1. Mission

> **KANG is an AI-native Personal Operating System that begins as a secretary, grows into a second brain, and ultimately becomes a lifelong thinking partner.**

---

## 2. North Star

> **Free Kang from operational work so he can spend his life creating things that matter.**

Every feature, every agent, every line of code is measured against this sentence. If a proposed feature does not free time, attention, or cognitive bandwidth for creation and growth, it does not belong in KANG.

---

## 3. Why KANG Exists

### 3.1 The problem

Kang's life runs on plans: competitions, deadlines, projects, study schedules, research threads, ideas, spiritual practices. Today, those plans live in fragments — Obsidian notes, Notion pages, browser tabs, memory, and intention.

The failure mode is specific and real:

> **If the system disappears, schedules and plans get ruined.**

Every missed competition deadline, every forgotten idea, every study plan that silently died — these are not intelligence failures. They are **infrastructure failures**. The operational layer of an ambitious life is fragile when it lives in scattered tools and human memory.

### 3.2 The insight

Highly effective people throughout history had secretaries, assistants, and chiefs of staff — not because they were lazy, but because **cognitive bandwidth is the scarcest resource of an ambitious person**. Every hour spent tracking deadlines is an hour not spent solving problems.

Until now, a personal secretary was a luxury reserved for executives. AI changes that.

### 3.3 Why now

- AI models are finally capable of reliable planning, memory, and tool use.
- Kang is at the highest-leverage point of life: the years before university, where habits, skills, and portfolio compound the most.
- Every year of delay is a year of lost compounding — for Kang's growth and for KANG's accumulated memory.

---

## 4. What KANG Is

### 4.1 An operating system, not an app

KANG is not a single AI.

KANG is an ecosystem.

It coordinates:

- **Memory** — what matters, remembered accurately across years
- **Agents** — specialists for competitions, learning, research, engineering, planning, critique, creativity, faith
- **Tools** — Obsidian, Notion, GitHub, calendars, browsers, models
- **Workflows** — the repeatable processes of Kang's life
- **Automation** — the operational work no human should waste attention on
- **Knowledge** — a growing, connected graph of everything learned and built

…into one coherent operating system for a human life.

A chatbot answers questions. An operating system **runs things**. That distinction governs everything: KANG schedules, monitors, remembers, initiates, and coordinates — whether or not anyone is typing into a chat box.

### 4.2 Three identities, in order

**The Secretary (first).** KANG's first job is operational: take care of a lot of stuff. Track every deadline. Maintain the daily plan. Watch the horizon for opportunities. Protect plans from decay. Prepare briefs before being asked. *Success: Kang never loses an opportunity or a plan because "nobody was tracking it."*

**The Second Brain (next).** KANG remembers what matters and connects it. Projects, research, ideas, notes — organized and linked, with deep Obsidian integration. Every project, competition, and note makes KANG smarter about how Kang works. *Success: "What do I know about X?" gets an honest, complete answer drawn from years of accumulated work.*

**The Thinking Partner (always becoming).** KANG optimizes for long-term capability, never mere convenience. It challenges weak ideas before judges, professors, or reality do. It teaches when Kang wants to learn and executes when Kang wants to build. *Success: Future Kang is measurably more capable than a version of Kang who never built KANG.*

### 4.3 Where it lives

KANG lives **alongside** Kang's existing tools — Windows, Chrome, VS Code, Obsidian, Notion, GitHub. It orchestrates them; it does not replace them. The closest real-world analogy is not software at all. It is a **chief of staff**.

---

## 5. What KANG Is Not

Clarity about non-goals prevents scope creep and wasted years.

| KANG is NOT | Because |
|---|---|
| A Windows replacement | It lives alongside the OS and orchestrates existing tools |
| Just another chatbot | Chat is one interface; the product is proactive operations + memory |
| A replacement for thinking | KANG amplifies thinking; Kang decides, learns, and creates |
| A general consumer product (for now) | Built for one user. Generalization is a possible future, not a goal |
| A spiritual authority | Scripture, prayer, and church hold that role. KANG only supports the practice |
| A dependency crutch | KANG must make Kang more independent and capable, not less |

## 6. The Five-Year Narrative

A vision is only real if you can picture life with it.

### Year 1 — The Secretary works

KANG runs daily. Every morning: today's quests, deadlines, schedule, priority tasks. Competition deadlines are never missed. Ideas are captured and filed. The Obsidian vault grows in an organized way. Kang trusts the system enough that losing it would hurt — which proves it matters.

### Year 2 — The memory compounds

KANG has a year of history: completed competitions, finished projects, abandoned ideas, study logs. It starts recognizing patterns: *"You always underestimate report-writing time by 2x."* *"This idea resembles the one you shelved in March — here's why that one failed."* The Critic Agent critiques with real data.

### Year 3 — The partner emerges

KANG proactively surfaces opportunities matched to Kang's demonstrated strengths. Research threads span months and KANG holds the context. Learning plans adapt based on what actually worked. The system feels less like a tool and more like a long-term collaborator who knows the whole story.

### Years 4–5 — The compounding payoff

Kang enters university (or a startup, or research) with: a multi-year portfolio, a knowledge graph of everything learned, an AI system calibrated to how Kang works better than anyone — and the engineering skill that came from *building* it. KANG itself becomes proof of capability: architecture, documentation, and a shipped product developed over years.

## 7. The Ten-Year Dream

Not a roadmap. A direction. The roadmap will change; the destination should not.

In ten years, KANG:

- **Runs on every device** Kang uses — desktop, laptop, phone — with seamless, encrypted sync.
- **Speaks and listens** — voice-first interaction for daily operations; typing is optional.
- **Sees** — vision capabilities for documents, whiteboards, screens, and the physical world.
- **Runs privately** — local models handle personal data whenever practical; the cloud is a choice, not a requirement.
- **Understands a decade of context** — ten years of projects, decisions, patterns, failures, and growth, recallable and connected.
- **Orchestrates hundreds of tools** — through a plugin ecosystem where any capability can be added without touching the core.
- **Has outlived its original stack** — AI providers, frameworks, and databases have been swapped multiple times, and KANG survived every migration because it was modular from day one.
- **Still passes the North Star test** — Kang spends his days creating things that matter, and KANG handles the rest.

When the roadmap gets confusing, this section is the compass.

## 8. Success Metrics

Vague visions die. These are the concrete tests.

### Year 1 — The Secretary test
- [ ] KANG runs daily and generates the morning plan without prompting.
- [ ] Zero missed competition or school deadlines that KANG knew about.
- [ ] Every project has a tracked workspace: goals, tasks, milestones, progress.
- [ ] The Obsidian vault is organized by KANG's conventions and Kang still trusts it.
- [ ] The honest test: **if KANG went offline for a week, Kang's plans would visibly suffer.**

### Year 3 — The Partner test
- [ ] KANG references past projects and patterns when critiquing new ideas.
- [ ] At least one competition result or project outcome is directly traceable to KANG's research, critique, or planning.
- [ ] Kang's learning is measurably faster (tracked via study plans, quizzes, spaced repetition data).

### Year 5 — The Life test
- [ ] KANG's memory spans years and remains accurate and useful.
- [ ] The codebase is still maintainable — a new contributor (or Future Kang) can understand it from the docs alone.
- [ ] Kang is demonstrably more capable *because of the process of building KANG*, not just using it.

### Anti-metrics — failure signals
- Kang stops thinking and just accepts KANG's outputs. → **Failure.**
- KANG becomes a novelty that's fun but doesn't change outcomes. → **Failure.**
- The system becomes so complex Kang stops maintaining it. → **Failure.**
- Building KANG crowds out the growth it was meant to serve. → **Failure.**

## 9. Constraints

Honest visions acknowledge limits.

1. **One developer, part-time.** Kang is a student with competitions, school, and a life. Scope must respect that. The roadmap will be aggressive in vision, conservative in sprint size.
2. **AI models will change.** Providers, prices, and capabilities will shift over ten years. KANG must never hard-depend on a single vendor.
3. **The secretary must ship first.** A grand multi-agent architecture that never runs is worth less than a simple daily planner that runs every morning. Version 0.1 must be small, real, and used daily.
4. **Motivation fluctuates.** The system must survive weeks of neglect and be easy to return to. Simplicity is a survival feature.
5. **KANG is a means, not the end.** If building KANG ever crowds out competitions, learning, faith, or creation, the priorities are inverted and must be corrected.

---

## 10. Closing Statement

KANG begins as a secretary: reliable, proactive, trusted with the plans that must not fail.

It grows into a second brain: the accumulated memory of everything Kang learns and builds.

It matures into a lifelong thinking partner: honest, challenging, and calibrated to one person's life across a decade.

And through the entire journey, two questions govern every decision:

> **"Does this free Kang to create things that matter?"**
>
> **"Will this genuinely help Future Kang become a better learner, engineer, researcher, innovator, creator, entrepreneur, and follower of Jesus?"**

If yes — build it well.
If no — redesign it.
